The 2026 premium was priced in 2024

Medicare's Part B premium reads like a current-year fee, but the price was locked two years earlier. The standard premium for 2026 is $202.90 a month, and about 8 percent of enrollees pay more, with the surcharge set by modified adjusted gross income from the tax return filed two years back. A household's 2026 premium is computed from its 2024 income. The bill cannot be changed in the year it is paid, because the only lever is income two years prior, which makes the premium a retroactive price on past decisions.

The surcharge tiers are wide and the amounts are flat inside them. A single filer at $112,000 of 2024 MAGI pays $284.10 a month in 2026, $974.40 a year over the standard, which works out to 0.87 percent of the whole MAGI. The span runs from $109,000 to $137,000 and the surcharge never moves inside it, so the rate on each additional dollar falls as income rises, the reverse of a tax bracket. At $136,999 the surcharge is still $974.40. At $137,001 the third tier starts, the premium goes to $405.80 a month, and the dollar that crossed the line carries $1,460.40 of new annual premium, a marginal rate of 146,040 percent on one dollar. The top tier adds $487.00 a month, $5,844 a year over standard.

For an early retiree the lag turns bridge-year planning into premium pricing. Income in the gap years sets premiums two years forward, and a Roth conversion sized without the table mis-prices its own edge. A conversion that lands MAGI at $137,500 instead of $136,000 pays the same tax rate on the last $1,500 and adds $1,460.40 to the 2026 premium, another 97 cents on those dollars in the first premium year alone. The EV check is the distance from the next tier line measured on the return that will be two years old when the bill arrives, and the conversion plan that stops at the bracket math stops short of the real price.
